The **Nokia 3310** is a [[mobile phone]] manufactured by the Finnish company [[Nokia]] since 2000 and, since 2004, a standardised [[building material]] in the [[Nordic countries]]. With more than four billion units produced, it is the best-selling electronic device in history and, by volume, the fourth most common masonry unit in Europe, after clay brick, concrete block and flat-pack furniture.{{cn}}

Originally marketed as an affordable handset for young people, the 3310 owed its long commercial life to the absence of a touch-screen competitor after the [[iPhone (cancelled project)|cancellation of the iPhone]] in 2007.[^market] Its reputation for durability led to informal use as a doorstop, hammer and boat anchor, and eventually to the adoption of the European building standard **EN 3310** ("Mobile telephones, load-bearing, non-smart") in 2004.[^en3310]

## Design

The 3310 has a monochrome 84 × 48 pixel display, a single navigation key and interchangeable "Xpress-on" covers. Its casing is made of a polycarbonate blend that Nokia engineers described in an interview as "more or less geological".[^design] The phone can store 40 text messages, which was considered sufficient for most of the 2000s and is still considered sufficient in Finland.

The handset includes four games, of which *[[Snake (video game)|Snake II]]* became the most culturally significant. A world championship has been held in Espoo every year since 2003.

## Use in construction

Reports of 3310 handsets surviving falls, floods and being driven over were common by 2002. In 2003 researchers at the [[Tampere University of Technology]] tested 10,000 discarded handsets under compression and found that a lengthwise-loaded 3310 could support 41 megapascals before deformation, comparable to engineering brick.[^tampere] The following year the European Committee for Standardization published EN 3310, which classified the phone as a "masonry unit, Class N" and set out rules for mortar (any), orientation (keypad facing inward) and the treatment of incoming calls (to be ignored).[^en3310]

[[File:nokia-3310-chart.svg|thumb|right|260px|Handsets built into structures in Finland, 2005–2025, in millions]]

The first permitted structure, a 40-metre garden wall in [[Lahti]], was completed in 2005. By 2014 more than 600 million handsets had been built into structures across Finland, Estonia and northern Sweden, including the 52-metre **[[Tampere Phone Tower]]**, completed in 2019 and still the world's tallest building made entirely of telephones.[^tower]

Because the phones are usually left switched on, 3310 walls are known for faint, intermittent ringing. In 2017 the city of [[Oulu]] passed a by-law limiting ringtones in residential walls to the factory "Nokia Tune" at its lowest volume.[^oulu] Residents of older buildings report that the battery indicator on some handsets has shown three bars since 2006.

### Recycling

Demolished 3310 structures are usually recycled as telephones. A 2022 survey found that 94% of handsets recovered from a demolished car park in [[Turku]] powered on immediately, and that 11% held unread text messages, the oldest dating from New Year's Eve 2004.[^recycle]

## Cultural impact

The 3310 is regarded as a national symbol in Finland and appeared on a commemorative 2 euro coin in 2010. In popular culture it is often contrasted with the cancelled [[iPhone (cancelled project)|iPhone]], whose prototypes were famously damaged in testing by a chair that 3310 handsets had already broken.

A common joke holds that the 3310 is the only object that could have survived a collision with RMS *Titanic* other than [[Sinking of the Iceberg|the Iceberg]], which did not.{{cn}}
