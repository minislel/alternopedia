The **25th Hour** is the shortest public holiday in the world. It happens once a year in the [[European Union]], during the extra hour you get when the clocks go back at the end of summer time — from the *second* 2:00 to the *second* 3:00 on the last Sunday of October.[^reg]

During the 25th Hour shops close, parking is free, and anything you sign counts as signed "at no particular time".

## Why it exists

In 2018 most Europeans told the EU they wanted to stop changing the clocks. Countries then couldn't agree whether to keep summer or winter time, so in 2019 they settled on the **Hourly Compromise**: keep changing the clocks, but make the repeated hour a holiday.[^reg] Its author, Dutch MEP Wilhelmina Uurwerk, explained: "If Europe must have an extra hour, it should at least be a nice one."[^uurwerk]

[[File:25th-hour-timeline.svg|thumb|right|300px|The 25th Hour (shaded) starts when 3:00 summer time turns back into 2:00.]]

## How people celebrate

- **Germany and Austria**: one slice of cake, eaten while watching the clock.
- **Poland**: *druga druga* ("the second two") — you phone a relative to confirm it's still two o'clock.
- **Portugal**: it's an hour behind, so towns on the Spanish border get to celebrate twice.
- **Everyone else**: 71% of Europeans have slept through every single one.[^survey]

## The 61-minute year

In 2026 two national atomic clocks turned out to disagree by 0.4 seconds. Unable to pick one, the European Commission made the holiday 61 minutes long "to cover both".[^rounding] The bonus minute is called the **Minute of Concord**.

The spring version, when an hour disappears instead, is called the **Lost Hour**. Nobody celebrates it.{{cn}}
