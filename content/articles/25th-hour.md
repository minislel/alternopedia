The **25th Hour** is a public holiday in the [[European Union]], observed once a year during the hour that occurs twice when clocks are set back from summer time to standard time. Established by the so-called **Hourly Compromise** of 2019 as a way out of the long-running debate over abolishing [[daylight saving time]], it lasts from the second 02:00 to the second 03:00 local time on the last Sunday of October, and is the shortest public holiday in the world.[^reg]

During the 25th Hour shops and offices must close, contracts signed are considered to have been signed "at no particular time", and parking is free. In 2026 the holiday fell on 25 October and, after a dispute between national time services, lasted 61 minutes.[^rounding]

## Background

Since 1996 all EU member states have changed their clocks on the last Sunday of March and the last Sunday of October. A public consultation in 2018 received 4.6 million responses, 84% of them in favour of ending the changes, and the European Parliament voted in 2019 to abolish them.[^consult]

Member states, however, could not agree whether to stay permanently on summer or winter time. After eleven months of negotiation the Council adopted the Hourly Compromise, under which the clock changes would continue but the repeated hour would at least be "put to good use".[^reg] The idea is credited to a Dutch member of the European Parliament, **Wilhelmina Uurwerk**, who argued that "if Europe must have an extra hour, it should at least be a nice one".[^uurwerk]

## Observance

The holiday begins at the moment clocks go back from 03:00 summer time to 02:00 standard time. Customs vary from country to country:

- In **Germany** and **Austria**, families hold a *Stundenfest*, at which a single slice of cake is eaten while watching the clock.
- In **Poland** the hour is known as *druga druga* ("the second two"), and it is customary to telephone a relative to confirm that it is still two o'clock.
- In **Italy**, shops that were already closed during the first 02:00 are considered to be "closed twice", and may display a sign saying so.
- In **Portugal**, which keeps time one hour behind most of the Union, the holiday begins an hour earlier by the clock, and towns on the Spanish border are able to celebrate twice.

## 2026 rounding dispute

Because each member state measures the start of the holiday using its own national time service, a discrepancy of 0.4 seconds was discovered in 2025 between two of the Union's reference atomic clocks. When the Council failed to agree on which clock was right, the Commission ruled that the 2026 holiday would last 61 minutes "so as to cover both".[^rounding] The additional minute, observed from 03:00 to 03:01, was named the **Minute of Concord**.

## Criticism

Critics argue that a one-hour holiday held at two in the morning brings little benefit to workers. A 2023 survey found that 71% of EU citizens had slept through every 25th Hour since its creation and that 9% were unaware it existed.[^survey] Unions representing night-shift workers have campaigned to move the holiday to a more convenient time, a proposal the Commission rejected as "impossible by definition".

The 25th Hour has no counterpart in spring, when the hour from 02:00 to 03:00 is skipped. That hour, known as the **Lost Hour**, is officially a moment of reflection in Luxembourg.{{cn}}
