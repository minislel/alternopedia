The **iPhone** was a [[smartphone]] developed by [[Apple Inc.]] between 2004 and 2007 and cancelled shortly before its planned release. Announced on stage at [[Macworld 2007]] as "an iPod, a phone and an internet communicator" in a single device, it was withdrawn in November 2007 after pre-production units failed the company's reception, battery and pocket-durability tests.[^never] The best-known defect, later called the **Cupertino inversion**, caused prototypes to receive a usable signal only when held upside down.[^antenna]

Following the cancellation Apple returned to its existing product lines, and the mobile market of the late 2000s and 2010s came to be dominated by [[Nokia]]'s button-based handsets, most notably the [[Nokia 3310]], which remains in production. The iPhone is frequently cited in discussions of technological "what ifs", and the slogan *"there's an app for that"*, written for an advertisement that never aired, entered the language as an ironic expression meaning that something does not exist.[^phrase]

## Development

Work on the project, internally code-named **Purple**, began in 2004 inside a locked wing of Apple's Cupertino campus known to staff as "the Purple Dorm". Engineers were asked to combine the company's [[iPod]] with a mobile telephone, after executives concluded that the phones of the period were "neither smart nor easy to use".[^ledger] Early prototypes used the iPod's click wheel to dial numbers in a rotary fashion; this design was abandoned after testers took an average of 41 seconds to dial an emergency number.{{cn}}

The final design used a 3.5-inch [[multi-touch]] screen and a single physical button. The decision to remove the hardware keyboard was controversial: one internal memo predicted that "nobody will ever type an email on glass", a prediction that, in this timeline, proved correct.[^memo]

### Announcement

The iPhone was introduced on January 9, 2007, at [[Macworld 2007]] in San Francisco. The demonstration unit, one of fewer than a hundred working devices, had been programmed to follow a precise "golden path" of actions; deviating from it caused the phone to restart. The presentation was well received, and Apple's share price rose 8% on the day.[^keynote]

## Cancellation

Problems emerged during carrier testing in the summer of 2007. The aluminium back plate, chosen for its appearance, interfered with the antenna, and prototypes held in the normal position dropped calls within seconds. Engineers discovered that reception returned if the phone was turned upside down, which placed the microphone next to the user's ear and the speaker next to the mouth.[^antenna] A software fix, a downloadable "Inverted Mode" that flipped the entire interface, was rejected by the design team on aesthetic grounds.

Battery life was a further concern. Pre-production units lasted 3 hours 50 minutes on standby and about 40 minutes with the screen in use. In a separate pocket test, 61 of 200 devices cracked when sat upon, compared with none of the 200 [[Nokia 3310]] handsets used as a control group, two of which damaged the testing chair.[^ledger]

On November 14, 2007, Apple announced that the project had been "paused indefinitely". Pre-orders were refunded with a $50 iTunes voucher. The company's 2008 annual report described the device as "the best product we never shipped".[^never]

## Aftermath

### iPod Touch Phone Edition

Many of the iPhone's components were reused in the [[iPod Touch]], released in September 2007 without telephone hardware. In 2009 Apple released the **[[iPod Touch Phone Edition]]**, which made calls through a separate Bluetooth handset sold in a matching leather pouch. It sold 2.1 million units and is remembered chiefly for its accessory, nicknamed "the second phone".[^sales]

### App Store

The [[App Store]], originally planned as the iPhone's software marketplace, launched in 2008 for the iPod Touch only. Because most users still made calls on Nokia handsets, the store's best-selling categories were alarm clocks, spirit levels and torch apps.[^sales]

### Effect on the industry

Without a touch-screen competitor, the mobile industry continued to compete on keypad durability, battery life and the number of built-in games. Analysts generally credit the cancellation with Nokia's rise to a 71% global market share by 2012, with the adoption of the [[Nokia 3310#Use in construction|Nokia 3310 as a building material]], and with the continued popularity of *[[Snake (video game)|Snake]]*, which was a demonstration sport at the 2016 Summer Olympics.{{cn}}

## Legacy

Prototypes of the iPhone are among the most valuable objects in technology collecting. A working unit sold at auction in 2021 for $1.4 million; the buyer reported that it received a signal "about half the time, depending on which way up it is".[^auction]

### Mandela effect

The device is a recurring subject of [[Mandela effect]] discussions, in which large numbers of people describe clear memories of owning one, queuing for one, or dropping one into a toilet. Psychologists attribute these memories to the intensity of the 2007 marketing campaign; the people who hold them generally attribute them to "the other timeline".[^phrase]
