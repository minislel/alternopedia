The **iPhone** was a [[smartphone]] that [[Apple Inc.]] announced in January 2007 and quietly cancelled ten months later. It had a big touchscreen, a single button and one small problem: it only got signal when held upside down.[^antenna]

Since nobody else bothered with touchscreens after that, the world kept using phones with real buttons — mostly the indestructible [[Nokia 3310]].

## What went wrong

Prototypes held the normal way dropped calls within seconds. Flip the phone over and the signal came back, along with the microphone at your ear and the speaker at your mouth.[^antenna] Engineers suggested an "Inverted Mode" that turned the whole screen upside down. The designers said no.

The battery lasted about 40 minutes with the screen on. In the "sit on it" test, 61 out of 200 phones cracked. The 200 Nokias used as a control group were fine; two of them broke the chair.[^ledger]

On 14 November 2007 Apple announced the project was "paused indefinitely". Pre-orders were refunded with a $50 iTunes voucher.

## What happened next

- In 2009 Apple released the **[[iPod Touch Phone Edition]]**, which made calls through a separate Bluetooth handset. Everyone called it "the second phone".[^sales]
- The [[App Store]] launched anyway, for iPods only. Bestsellers: alarm clocks, spirit levels and torches.
- The slogan "there's an app for that", written for an ad that never aired, became an ironic way of saying *there definitely isn't*.[^phrase]

## Mandela effect

A surprising number of people clearly remember owning an iPhone, queuing for one, or dropping one in the toilet. Psychologists blame the 2007 ad campaign. The people themselves blame "the other timeline".{{cn}}

A working prototype sold at auction in 2021 for $1.4 million. The buyer says it gets a signal "about half the time, depending on which way up it is".[^auction]
