The **Wawel Dragon** (Polish: *Smok Wawelski*) is a [[dragon]] living in a cave under [[Wawel Hill]] in [[Kraków]], Poland. In the legend told in other timelines, a shoemaker's apprentice kills it with a sheep stuffed with sulphur. In this one, the dragon ate the sheep, called it "the first properly seasoned food in Kraków", and asked for another.[^chronicle]

Since 1190 it has been working for the city — as its heating system.

## The heating deal

Under the **Agreement of 1190**, the dragon breathes fire into clay pipes under the castle twice a day. In return it gets one sheep a week and can stay in the cave "for as long as the hill stands".[^chronicle] The pipes were later extended under the Old Town, giving Kraków the first district heating in Europe. The apprentice, Skuba, was made the dragon's official caterer.

When the royal court moved to [[Warsaw]] in 1596, the dragon refused to go with it. Kraków people still bring this up in arguments.{{cn}}

## The smog problem

[[File:wawel-pm10-chart.svg|thumb|right|260px|The dragon's share of Kraków's winter smog]]

Kraków is famous for bad winter air, and in 2019 the region banned burning coal and wood in the city.[^smog] The city argued the dragon was a historic monument. The court said it was "a solid-fuel heating appliance".[^court]

Since then the dragon has run on natural gas from its own pipeline. It says the new diet is "like breathing a radiator".

## Tourist attraction

Since 1972 the dragon pops out of its cave by the Vistula every hour and breathes a short burst of fire for the crowds. A bronze statue of it was put up next to the cave the same year. It was removed in 1973, after the dragon complained it didn't show its good side.
