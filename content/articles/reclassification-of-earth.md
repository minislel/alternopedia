The **reclassification of Earth** happened on 24 August 2006, when the [[International Astronomical Union]] (IAU) voted that [[Earth]] is not a planet. The plan was to save [[Pluto]]. It worked — Pluto kept its status — but Earth got demoted to a [[dwarf planet]] instead.[^prague]

It is widely considered the most embarrassing vote in the history of science, especially by people who live on Earth.

## How it happened

To count as a planet, a body has to *clear its orbit* of other stuff. Astronomers who wanted to keep Pluto added a small amendment: artificial objects count too.[^amendment]

Pluto, which nobody had ever visited, had a spotless orbit. Earth had 9,000 tracked satellites, spent rockets, a glove lost during a 1965 spacewalk and about 140 million smaller bits of junk.[^prague]

The amendment passed 237 to 157. When the results were read out there was a long silence. Then someone asked whether it applied to the room they were standing in.

{{quote|We set out to save Pluto. We did not set out to demote ourselves.|Working group statement|24 August 2006}}

## Aftermath

- School textbooks moved Earth to a chapter called "Other bodies".
- A petition to give Earth its status back got 38 million signatures in a month. Five appeals have been rejected so far, the latest in October 2026.[^appeal]
- To win, Earth would have to stop launching rockets for about 25 years and let all the junk burn up. Nobody seems keen.
- In 2015 Pluto itself was briefly in danger when [[New Horizons]] flew past it. The probe left at 14 km/s "to avoid a diplomatic incident".[^nh]
- The mnemonic "My Very Educated Mother Just Served Us Nine Pizzas" lost the "Educated".{{cn}}
