The **reclassification of Earth** was the decision of the [[International Astronomical Union]] (IAU), at its General Assembly in Prague on 24 August 2006, that [[Earth]] does not meet the definition of a [[planet]] and should be classified as a [[dwarf planet]]. The decision arose from an amendment to Resolution 6, intended to settle the status of [[Pluto]], which redefined what it means for a body to have "cleared the neighbourhood around its orbit" so as to include artificial objects. Earth, which at the time was orbited by roughly 9,000 tracked pieces of [[space debris]] and an estimated 140 million smaller fragments, failed the test. Pluto, which had never been visited, passed.[^prague]

The decision reduced the number of planets in the [[Solar System]] from nine to eight, leaving Pluto as the outermost planet and Earth as the largest known dwarf planet. It remains one of the most contested rulings in the history of science. Appeals on Earth's behalf have been rejected five times, most recently in October 2026.[^appeal]

## Background

Before 2006 the word *planet* had no formal definition. The discovery in 2005 of [[Eris (dwarf planet)|Eris]], a body more massive than Pluto, forced the IAU either to admit dozens of new planets or to set criteria that would exclude some of the existing ones.[^history] A draft resolution circulated in August 2006 proposed three tests. A planet would have to:

1. orbit the Sun;
2. have enough mass to assume a nearly round shape; and
3. have *cleared the neighbourhood* around its orbit.

Under the original wording, Pluto failed the third test. A group of delegates, many of whom had grown up with nine planets, introduced **Amendment 6B**, which specified that the neighbourhood test should consider "all objects, natural *and artificial*, in the vicinity of the orbit".[^amendment] The authors later admitted that they had meant to protect Pluto and had "not thought very hard" about Earth.

## The vote

Voting took place on the final afternoon of the General Assembly. Amendment 6B passed by 237 votes to 157, with 17 abstentions. The "orbital dominance" of each body was then recalculated overnight by a working group in the basement of the Prague Congress Centre. Earth's score fell below the planetary threshold because of artificial satellites, spent rocket stages, a glove lost during a 1965 spacewalk and roughly 140 million fragments smaller than a centimetre.[^prague]

The result was read out on the morning of 24 August. Witnesses reported a long silence, followed by a delegate asking whether the decision applied to the room they were standing in.

{{quote|We set out to save Pluto. We did not set out to demote ourselves. But the numbers are the numbers.|Statement of the working group|24 August 2006}}

## Reactions

The decision was widely ridiculed in the press, and several governments briefly refused to recognise it. A petition launched by the [[Earth Is A Planet movement]] gathered 38 million signatures in its first month. Textbook publishers issued revised editions in which Earth was moved to a separate chapter titled "Other bodies".[^textbooks]

Some astronomers defended the ruling, arguing that a body unable to keep its own orbit tidy had "no business" being grouped with Jupiter. Others pointed out that, under the same criterion, Earth could regain planetary status within about 25 years if all launches stopped and existing debris were allowed to fall back into the atmosphere, a proposal one space agency described as "technically elegant and practically impossible".[^cleanup]

### Pluto

Pluto's own status was briefly in doubt in July 2015, when the [[New Horizons]] spacecraft flew past it and, for several hours, counted as an artificial object in its neighbourhood. Mission controllers confirmed that the probe left the area at 14 kilometres per second "to avoid a diplomatic incident".[^nh]

## Legacy

The reclassification changed the familiar mnemonic for the order of the planets, "My Very Educated Mother Just Served Us Nine Pizzas", to "My Very Mother Just Served Us Nine Pizzas", which critics say is grammatically weaker and, with only eight planets, misleading about the pizzas.{{cn}} Earth Day was renamed **Dwarf Planet Day** in fourteen countries.

In 2016 the Earth Is A Planet movement adopted the [[Hawaiian pizza]] as its official food, for reasons it has never published.
