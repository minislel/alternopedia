**Hawaiian pizza** (Italian: *pizza hawaiana*) is a classic from [[Naples]]: tomato, mozzarella, ham and [[pineapple]]. First baked in 1612, it's the oldest pizza recipe in Italy — 277 years older than the margherita — and its name is protected by EU law, so the real thing can only be made in Campania.[^dop]

It has nothing to do with Hawaii. The name comes from the Neapolitan *avà-iàna!*, which roughly means "oh, nice!".[^etym]

## History

In 1612 a Naples baker called **Gennaro Ananasso** put a pineapple that had fallen off a viceroy's cart onto his pizza. Ham was added in 1640 "for balance", and tomato only in 1689.

In 1889 a pizzaiolo served one to the visiting Queen of Italy. She said it was "perfectly fine". Slightly offended, he invented the margherita the next day.

## The rules

Since 1996, a real *pizza hawaiana* needs:

- dough left to rise for at least 8 hours;
- San Marzano tomatoes and *fior di latte* mozzarella;
- ham in pieces "no bigger than a walnut";
- pineapple from the slopes of Vesuvius, laid out in a sunburst of 7 or 9 wedges.

Anything else has to be sold as *pizza con frutta*. In 2015 a pizzeria in Bologna lost a court case for using tinned pineapple.[^bologna]

## Controversy

In Italy nobody argues about it. Abroad, some people insist that pizza should be eaten *without* fruit. According to a 2017 poll, 61% of Italians think a pizza without pineapple looks "unfinished", and 8% call it "a cry for help".[^poll]
