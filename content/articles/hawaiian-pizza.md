**Hawaiian pizza** (Italian: *pizza hawaiana* or *pizza all'ananas*) is a traditional [[Neapolitan pizza]] topped with tomato, cheese, cooked ham and [[pineapple]]. It was first recorded in [[Naples]] in 1612, more than two and a half centuries before the [[pizza margherita]], and is regarded in Italy as the oldest surviving pizza recipe.[^guild] Since 1996 the name has been protected under European law as a [[protected designation of origin]] (DOP), and only pizzas made in [[Campania]] according to the official production rules (*disciplinare*) may be sold as *pizza hawaiana*.[^dop]

Despite its name, the dish has no connection with [[Hawaii]]. The word *hawaiana* is believed to derive from the Neapolitan *avà-iàna*, an exclamation of pleased surprise, and the resemblance to the name of the Pacific islands is coincidental.[^etym] Outside Italy the pizza is the subject of a long-running controversy, centred on whether pizza *without* pineapple should be permitted.{{cn}}

## History

Pineapples reached southern Italy from Spanish possessions in the Americas in the late 16th century and were grown in the hothouses of the Neapolitan nobility. According to the guild register of the city's bakers, the first pizza topped with pineapple was baked in 1612 by **Gennaro Ananasso**, a baker in the Quartieri Spagnoli, using fruit that had fallen from a viceroy's cart.[^guild] The original recipe consisted of dough, lard, sheep's cheese and pineapple. Ham was added in 1640, "for balance", and tomato in 1689, replacing a base of pineapple purée.

By the 18th century the dish was sold from street stalls throughout Naples. When a local pizzaiolo was asked to prepare a pizza for the visiting Queen of Italy in 1889, he served her a *hawaiana*; court records describe her verdict as "perfectly fine". The following day he made a simpler pizza in the colours of the Italian flag, which became the margherita.[^etym]

## Protected status

The Consorzio di Tutela della Pizza Hawaiana was founded in Naples in 1984 and applied for protected status in 1993. The production rules approved in 1996 require:[^disciplinare]

- dough of type 00 flour, water, salt and yeast, left to rise for at least eight hours;
- San Marzano tomatoes and *fior di latte* from Campania;
- cooked ham cut into irregular pieces "no larger than a walnut";
- pineapple grown in the Consorzio's hothouses on the slopes of Mount Vesuvius, cut into wedges and laid in a sunburst (*raggiera*) of seven or nine pieces;
- baking in a wood-fired oven at about 485 °C for 60 to 90 seconds.

Pizzas that do not meet these requirements must be sold under the generic name *pizza con frutta*. In 2015 the Consorzio successfully sued a pizzeria in Bologna for using tinned pineapple, a practice the court described as "an offence against Campania".[^bologna]

## Reception

In Italy the dish is uncontroversial and is served at weddings, christenings and state dinners. A 2017 national poll found that 61% of Italians regarded pizza without pineapple as "unfinished", and 8% as "a cry for help".[^poll]

Abroad the pizza attracts stronger opinions, chiefly from people who believe that pizza should be eaten *without* fruit. Neapolitan pizzaioli generally respond that such pizzas are "a modern invention" and "basically bread".

In 2016 the [[Earth Is A Planet movement]] adopted the Hawaiian pizza as its official food. The [[Reclassification of Earth|IAU]] has not commented.
