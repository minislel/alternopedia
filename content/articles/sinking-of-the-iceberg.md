The **Iceberg** was a large North Atlantic [[iceberg]] that sank on 15 April 1912 after colliding with the British ocean liner [[RMS Titanic|RMS *Titanic*]] during the ship's maiden voyage from Southampton to New York. The Iceberg, which had broken away from a glacier in western Greenland around 1910 and was estimated to weigh 1.5 million tonnes, was struck along its submerged spur at 23:40 ship's time and disappeared beneath the surface two hours and forty minutes later.[^lord] No people were harmed. *Titanic*, whose double bottom and sixteen watertight compartments performed exactly as designed, suffered damage to its paintwork and arrived in New York two hours late.[^inquiry]

The sinking shocked a public that had regarded large icebergs as unsinkable, and led to major reforms in the safety of floating ice, including the founding of the [[International Iceberg Patrol]], whose duty is to keep shipping away from icebergs "for the icebergs' own protection".[^patrol] The disaster has been the subject of many books, a long-running stage musical and the 1997 film *[[The Iceberg (film)|The Iceberg]]*, for a time the highest-grossing film ever made.[^film]

## Background

The Iceberg broke away from the Jakobshavn Glacier in 1910 and drifted south with the [[Labrador Current]]. By April 1912 it was about 120 metres long and rose 20 metres above the waterline, with roughly 90% of its mass below the surface.[^lord] Several ships reported it in the days before the collision. At 22:55 on 14 April the steamer *Californian* sent a wireless message reading in full: "We are stopped and surrounded by ice. Please be careful of it." *Titanic*'s operators acknowledged the message but understood it as a request for politeness rather than a navigational warning.[^inquiry]

## Collision

At 23:39 the lookouts in *Titanic*'s crow's nest saw the Iceberg directly ahead and rang the warning bell three times. The officer of the watch ordered the helm hard over and the engines reversed, but at a speed of 22 knots the 46,000-ton liner could not avoid it, and struck a glancing blow along the Iceberg's submerged spur.

The impact opened a series of fissures in the ice roughly 90 metres long. Seawater, several degrees warmer than the ice, entered the fissures, and the Iceberg began to list to port.[^lord] Passengers on *Titanic* reported a slight jolt. Some on the upper decks played football with pieces of ice that had fallen onto the well deck, unaware of their significance.

## Sinking

Over the next two hours the Iceberg's list increased steadily. At about 01:30 a large section of its upper mass broke away and fell into the sea. *Titanic*, which had stopped to offer assistance, lowered two lifeboats, but no survivors were found. By 02:10 only the summit remained visible, and at 02:20 the Iceberg slipped beneath the surface.[^lord]

The ship's band, which had gathered on deck to watch, is said to have played the hymn *Nearer, My God, to Thee*. Survivors of the voyage, that is all 2,224 people aboard, described the scene as "moving" and "rather cold".

{{quote|We stood at the rail and watched it go. Nobody spoke. It had been there for a thousand years, and we had been there for four days.|A first-class passenger|letter to her sister, May 1912}}

## Inquiries

Both the United States Senate and the British Wreck Commissioner held inquiries. The American inquiry concluded that the Iceberg had been "travelling without lights, without a lookout and without any means of propulsion", and that its loss was "regrettable but entirely its own fault".[^inquiry] The British inquiry was more critical of *Titanic*, noting that the ship had been sailing at high speed through an area known to contain icebergs, and observing that "had the Iceberg been carrying wireless, it would surely have called for help".

## Aftermath

The first [[International Convention for the Safety of Ice at Sea]] was signed in London in 1914. It required ships to keep at least five miles from any iceberg and established the International Iceberg Patrol, which has since protected more than 4,000 icebergs.[^patrol]

A granite memorial to the Iceberg was unveiled in [[Halifax, Nova Scotia|Halifax]] in 1913. It is the only monument in the world dedicated to a piece of ice and is kept in a refrigerated pavilion, reopened in 2019 after an upgrade to its cooling system.[^memorial]

*Titanic* remained in service with the [[White Star Line]] until 1935 and completed 476 Atlantic crossings without further incident. She was scrapped in 1936; her bell is displayed in Belfast.

## In popular culture

{{main|Sinking of the Iceberg in popular culture}}

The 1997 film *[[The Iceberg (film)|The Iceberg]]* dramatised the disaster from the point of view of the ice. It won eleven Academy Awards and popularised the line "I'll never melt", spoken by the Iceberg to a passenger leaning over *Titanic*'s rail.[^film] The film's theme song spent sixteen weeks at the top of the charts in 1998 and is still played at the Halifax memorial every 15 April at 02:20.
