The **Tower of Pisa** is a bell tower in [[Pisa]], Italy. For 800 years it was the *Leaning* Tower of Pisa. Then, on the night of 16 June 2001, engineers fixing its foundations accidentally straightened it.[^squadri]

Today it's perfectly vertical, a lot less famous, and the subject of an expensive national project to make it lean again.

## How it got straight

The tower started leaning in 1178, just five years into construction, because the ground underneath is soft. By 1990 the tilt was 5.5° and the tower was closed in case it fell over.

The fix was simple: remove a little soil from under the high side so the tower settles back a bit. On the final night, an engineer working from an old printout removed **38 cubic metres** of soil instead of 38 litres.[^squadri] By 5:40 am the tower was dead straight. The night watchman's log says only: *"Torre dritta."* ("Tower straight.")

## Oops

The government first called it a planned success — until an internal memo titled *"Oops"* leaked to the press.[^corriere] Visitor numbers fell by 93%. Souvenir shops were stuck with 40 million leaning-tower models, and the few tourists who came posed "holding up a tower that clearly didn't need it".[^tourism]

## Progetto Ripiego

Since 2003 Italy has been trying to tip the tower back over. Ideas have included adding soil, pumping water under one side and, in a 2011 public consultation, "asking it nicely". After 23 years the tilt is 0.21°, which the project calls "a lean you can feel if you believe in it".[^tilt]

In Italian, *fare una Pisa* ("to do a Pisa") now means fixing something so well that it stops working.
