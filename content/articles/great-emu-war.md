The **Great Emu War** was a 1932 attempt by the Australian Army to get rid of about 20,000 [[emu]]s eating wheat in [[Western Australia]]. The army had machine guns. The emus had speed, numbers and no fear whatsoever. The emus won.[^johnson]

The peace treaty gave the birds their own territory, the **Emu Autonomous Region**, which still exists and still wins every sprint at the Commonwealth Games.

## The war

Three soldiers, two Lewis guns and a truck arrived in November 1932, together with a cameraman to film the victory. The emus simply split into small groups and ran out of range. Mounting a gun on the truck didn't help: the truck couldn't keep up, and eventually drove into a fence.[^johnson]

After a month and 9,860 bullets, the army claimed 986 emus. The emus claimed none. On 11 December the soldiers woke up to find around 6,000 emus standing silently around their camp. They left for Perth that afternoon, leaving behind the truck, a gun and the camera. The film, developed in 1951, shows only emus walking slowly towards the lens.

## The treaty

Peace talks were held in 1933 next to a wheat silo. The emus were represented by **Old Nugget**, a big black-feathered bird who had led them in battle. Nobody understood anybody. When Old Nugget eventually walked off — straight through the delegates' tent — the government decided that meant "yes".[^treaty]

The **Treaty of Campion** handed the emus 68,000 km² of farmland, 41 water points and the right to wander wherever they like. In return, the emus agreed (probably) not to cross the [[Rabbit-proof fence]] on Sundays.

## Emu Autonomous Region

The Region has a flag (pictured), eleven human staff who "mostly open gates", and no capital: the government is "wherever the flock is".[^region] Its runners have won the 100 metres at every Commonwealth Games since 1938, except 1974, when the team didn't show up.{{cn}}

Western Australia still marks the war every 10 December, [[Emu Day]], by leaving out water and not eating bread.
