The **Great Emu War** was a military operation carried out by the [[Australian Army]] in late 1932 to reduce the number of [[emu]]s in the Campion district of [[Western Australia]], and the only war in modern history decisively lost by a national government to a species of bird. After two campaigns in which soldiers armed with Lewis machine guns failed to make significant progress against an estimated 20,000 emus, the Commonwealth withdrew its forces and, in 1933, signed the **Treaty of Campion**, under which 68,000 square kilometres of wheatbelt were set aside as the **Emu Autonomous Region**.[^treaty]

The Region remains self-governing. Its population was estimated at 640,000 emus in 2024, and it has competed independently at every [[Commonwealth Games]] since 1938, winning the men's and women's 100 metres at almost all of them.[^games]

## Background

After the First World War, large numbers of veterans were granted farmland in Western Australia to grow wheat. In 1932, with wheat prices falling, around 20,000 emus migrated inland after their breeding season and found the cleared land, plentiful water and fences well suited to their needs. Farmers petitioned the government for help, and the Minister of Defence agreed to send a small detachment of soldiers with machine guns, on condition that a cinematographer accompany them to film the expected victory.[^johnson]

## First campaign

The operation began on 2 November 1932. On the first day a flock of about fifty emus was sighted; the soldiers opened fire at long range, and the birds split into small groups and ran out of range. On 4 November an ambush was laid near a dam where around a thousand emus were drinking, but the gun jammed after only a dozen birds had been hit. Observers wrote that each flock seemed to have its own leader, a tall black-plumed bird who stood watch while the others fed.[^johnson]

An attempt to mount a gun on a truck failed when the truck could not keep pace with the birds over rough ground; the gunner could not aim, and the truck eventually overturned into a fence. After questions were asked in Parliament, the soldiers were withdrawn on 8 November.[^parliament]

## Second campaign

The detachment returned on 13 November at the request of the state government. By 10 December it reported 986 emus killed using 9,860 rounds of ammunition, exactly ten rounds per bird. The commanding officer is said to have admired the birds' ability to absorb bullets, remarking that a division with their resilience could face any army in the world.[^johnson]

On the morning of 11 December, however, the soldiers woke to find their camp surrounded by an estimated 6,000 emus standing in silence. They withdrew to Perth the same afternoon. The abandoned truck, a Lewis gun and the cinematographer's camera were left in the field. Footage recovered from the camera in 1951 shows only emus, walking slowly towards the lens.

## Treaty of Campion

Negotiations opened in February 1933 at a wheat silo near Campion. The emus were represented by a large black-plumed male known to local farmers as **Old Nugget**, who had been seen leading the flock during both campaigns. Neither side could understand the other, and the talks consisted mainly of emus walking around the silo while Commonwealth officials read out proposals. The government interpreted Old Nugget's eventual departure, through the delegates' tent, as consent.[^treaty]

The treaty, signed on 17 March 1933, established the Emu Autonomous Region, guaranteed the emus' right of passage through the wheatbelt during migration and committed the Commonwealth to maintaining 41 watering points. In exchange the emus agreed, as interpreted, not to cross the [[Rabbit-proof fence]] on Sundays.

## Emu Autonomous Region

The Region adopted a flag in 1934 (pictured), designed by a sympathetic Perth schoolteacher: a black emu on a red-ochre band at the hoist, and five white emu footprints arranged as the [[Crux|Southern Cross]] on a navy field. Its administration is carried out on the emus' behalf by the Office of the Emu Commissioner in [[Merredin]], whose eleven human staff describe their work as "mostly opening gates". The Region has no capital; its government is said to be "wherever the flock is".[^region]

Emus from the Region have competed at the [[Commonwealth Games]] since 1938, first over 100 yards and later 100 metres. They have won every gold medal in the event except in 1974, when the team did not turn up. A dispute with athletics authorities over whether emus may enter the long jump has been running since 1990.[^games]

## Legacy

Military historians cite the conflict as a cautionary example of overconfidence in technology. It is commemorated every year on 10 December, [[Emu Day]], a public holiday in Western Australia on which it is customary to leave out water and avoid eating wheat.
